# 1. Problem Statement

## 1.1 Context
In metropolitan areas such as Mumbai, solid waste generation exceeds 10,000 metric tons per day. Rapid urban density, extensive commercial activities, and informal dumping have created recurring waste hotspots along public roads, railway tracks, beaches, and drainage channels. 

## 1.2 Core Issues
1. **Absence of Centralized Citizen-Authority Interface:**
   Citizens encountering unattended waste heaps lack an accessible, real-time mechanism to lodge complaints with accurate geographical markers. Most complaints are routed through traditional paper registers or localized ward offices without tracking numbers.

2. **Inadequate Source Segregation:**
   Municipal solid waste in Indian cities frequently arrives at dumping grounds in an unsegregated condition. Wet biodegradable waste mixes with single-use plastics, toxic electronic waste (e-waste), and construction debris, preventing scientific composting and recycling.

3. **Lack of Operational Transparency:**
   Citizens are often unaware of whether reported garbage has been inspected, assigned to a cleanup crew, or successfully cleared. This absence of feedback discourages civic participation.

4. **Resource Misallocation for Municipal Crews:**
   Without visual evidence, categorical sorting, and geospatial coordinates, municipal sanitation inspectors waste valuable time conducting physical scouting before dispatching appropriate equipment (e.g., composting trucks vs. hazardous e-waste recyclers).

## 1.3 Problem Definition
To design, develop, and test an open-source, full-stack web application—**EcoTrack: Smart Waste Management and Segregation System**—that enables citizens to report waste hotspots with category tags, landmarks, GPS coordinates, and photographs, while empowering municipal administrators to triage, assign, update, and audit cleanup progress transparently using open-source mapping and analytics tools without reliance on proprietary paid APIs.

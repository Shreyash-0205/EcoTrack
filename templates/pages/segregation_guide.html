Comprehensive Waste Segregation Guide

Learn how to classify, separate, and dispose of household and commercial waste to promote resource recovery and protect sanitation workers.

Local Municipal Guidance Notice

Waste segregation practices, bin color-coding standards, and collection schedules vary across municipalities and local urban bodies (e.g., Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai / MCGM, Pune Municipal Corporation, or Navi Mumbai Municipal Corporation). While the guidelines below follow India's Solid Waste Management Rules (2016) and Swachh Bharat guidelines, please verify specific collection timings, bulk waste protocols, and sanitary waste wrapping norms with your local ward office.

1. Wet & Biodegradable Waste

Organic & Compostable Matter • Standard Green Bin
What Goes In:
  • Fruit and vegetable peels, vegetable trimmings, spoiled produce.
  • Leftover cooked food, curry residue, plate scrapings.
  • Eggshells, tea bags, coffee grounds, coconut shells.
  • Garden leaves, grass clippings, wilted flowers, and festive organic offerings.
  • Meat bones and seafood residue (where acceptable by municipal composter).
What Must NOT Go In:
  • Plastic bags or plastic container linings (never dispose food inside tied plastic pouches).
  • Diapers, sanitary napkins, pet poop, or medical bandages.
Practical Eco-Tip: Drain excess gravy or liquid before placing in the bin to minimize foul odor and fly breeding. Consider home composting or apartment vermicomposting to generate nutrient-rich garden soil.

2. Dry & Recyclable Waste

Inorganics & Non-Biodegradables • Standard Blue Bin
What Goes In:
  • Newspapers, office paper, notebooks, envelopes, and receipts.
  • Cardboard boxes, cereal boxes, corrugated packaging cartons.
  • Metal cans, aluminum foil (clean), tin lids, iron bottle caps.
  • Glass beverage bottles, food jars, cosmetic glassware (unbroken).
  • Clean textiles, cotton rags, old clothes, worn footwear.
What Must NOT Go In:
  • Food-soiled greasy pizza boxes or oil-drenched paper.
  • Broken mirror glass, ceramics, or light bulbs.
Practical Eco-Tip: Flatten cardboard boxes to save bin volume. Ensure paper and cans are clean and completely dry to prevent molding in transit.

3. Plastic Waste

Polymers, Films & Packaging • Dedicated Recyclables Bin
What Goes In:
  • Type 1 (PET): Mineral water bottles, soda bottles, clear juice containers.
  • Type 2 (HDPE): Shampoo bottles, detergent cans, milk gallon jugs.
  • Type 5 (PP): Food take-away containers, yogurt tubs, bottle caps.
  • Clean milk pouches and oil pouches (washed, snipped, and dried).
  • Bubble wrap, protective air pouches, clean grocery polybags.
What Must NOT Go In:
  • Multilayer metallized plastic snack bags (chips packets) without local MLP collection.
  • Unrinsed milk/curd pouches that contain liquid residue.
Practical Eco-Tip: When opening milk pouches, do not snip off the small corner completely. Leave it attached to the main packet so small plastic bits do not choke urban storm drains.

4. Electronic Waste (E-waste)

Hazardous Circuitry & Batteries • Authorized E-Waste Centers
What Goes In:
  • Obsolete mobile phones, tablets, chargers, and USB cords.
  • Computer motherboards, hard disks, RAM sticks, power cables.
  • Keyboards, mice, webcams, headphones, and Bluetooth earbuds.
  • Rechargeable lithium-ion cells, laptop batteries, button batteries.
  • Broken home appliances: toasters, blenders, electric kettles.
Critical Safety Warning:
  • NEVER discard e-waste into normal municipal garbage trucks or burn it.
  • Electronics contain heavy metals (lead, mercury, cadmium) that leach into groundwater.
Practical Eco-Tip: Utilize authorized State Pollution Control Board (MPCB) electronic collection centers or retailer take-back kiosks in electronic retail stores.

5. Mixed, Domestic Hazardous & Construction Waste

Sanitary, Chemical & Construction Residue • Secure Packaging
Domestic Hazardous Waste:
  • Expired pharmaceutical medicines, syrup bottles, blister packs.
  • Paint cans, varnish thinners, chemical solvents.
  • Insecticide sprays, mosquito repellant coils, rodent poison.
  • Fluorescent tube lights and mercury-containing thermometers.
Sanitary & Biomedical Protocols:
  • Diapers, sanitary napkins, and medical dressings must be securely wrapped in newspaper or biodegradable pouches marked with a red cross.
  • Construction & Demolition (C&D) debris (concrete rubble, broken tiles, plaster) must be scheduled via municipal bulk pickup hotlines.
